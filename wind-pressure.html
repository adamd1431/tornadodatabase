Tropical cyclone tools CKZ / ADT

Wind ↔ pressure

Estimate central pressure from sustained wind, or work back from pressure. Account for the cyclone’s latitude, size, motion, and surrounding pressure.

?CKZ tutorialWhat to enter, where to find it, and a worked example+

CKZ estimates how a cyclone’s maximum wind relates to its central pressure, accounting for its surroundings and structure. Start with an advisory or best-track record for one observation time. Find outer-isobar values in a storm analysis or sea-level pressure chart; the advisory’s central pressure is a different quantity.

Use the calculator in four steps

  1. Choose CKZ and your starting value

    Select Wind → pressure for a known 1-minute sustained wind at 10 m, or Pressure → wind for a known central pressure. Use observations from the same time; gusts and 10-minute winds are different inputs.

  2. Set latitude and environmental pressure

    Enter north as positive and south as negative: 20° S becomes −20. The pressure field is Pe, used directly in the equation. The CKZ convention is outermost closed-isobar pressure (POCI) + 2 mb: a POCI of 1008 mb means entering 1010.

  3. Enter the storm’s size

    R34 is the radius of 34-knot winds, measured outward from the center. Average the reported nonzero NE, SE, SW, and NW radii. Use nautical miles, or choose km. If you have the radius of the outermost closed isobar (ROCI) instead, select ROCI; this calculator estimates R34 from it.

  4. Add forward motion and read the result

    Forward speed is how fast the storm’s center moves, in knots. Results update immediately. The pressure deficit is environmental pressure minus central pressure. Open Calculation details to follow the numbers.

How do I average R34?

For radii of 120, 100, 80, and 100 nmi, enter (120 + 100 + 80 + 100) ÷ 4 = 100 nmi. A reported zero is left out of the average; a missing quadrant is unknown. Enter 0 only when there are no 34-knot winds.

Before using a result

Use an analyzed environmental pressure and radius when available; the starting values are examples. R34 is a radius, not the eye diameter or radius of maximum wind. Pressure → wind can show several solutions. An estimate from the relationship can differ from an observed storm.

About the methods

CKZ (2009) uses the published low-latitude equation below 18° and the standard equation at 18° and above. Storm motion is editable. ADT / McIDAS-V reproduces SSEC’s documented CKZ conversion, including its 11-knot motion default and weak-system pressure adjustment.

All wind results are 1-minute sustained estimates. The CI option starts from an existing intensity number; satellite imagery is needed to derive CI itself. Pressure → wind numerically inverts the selected equation. More than one solution can exist; matches in the 25–250 knot calculation range are shown, with a minimum of 34 knots when a gale radius is present. These are empirical estimates; real storms can depart from the relationship.
