Wind ↔ pressure

Estimate cyclone wind and pressure with CKK 2025, Brown, CKZ, or ADT. Convert Dvorak intensity numbers, or use SCH for central pressure estimates from station observations.

?CKZ R34 tutorialWhat to enter, where to find it, and a worked example+

CKZ estimates how a cyclone’s maximum wind relates to its central pressure, accounting for its surroundings and structure. Start with an advisory or best-track record for one observation time. Find outer-isobar values in a storm analysis or sea-level pressure chart; the advisory’s central pressure is a different quantity.

Use the calculator in four steps

  1. Choose CKZ · Published R34 equations

    Select Wind → pressure for a known 1-minute sustained wind at 10 m, or Pressure → wind for a known central pressure. Use observations from the same time; gusts and 10-minute winds are different inputs.

  2. Set latitude and environmental pressure

    Enter north as positive and south as negative: 20° S becomes −20. The pressure field is Pe, used directly in the equation. The CKZ convention is outermost closed-isobar pressure (POCI) + 2 mb: a POCI of 1008 mb means entering 1010.

  3. Enter the storm’s size

    TS-force radius (R34) is the distance from the center to the edge of tropical-storm-force winds (34 kt). Average the reported nonzero NE, SE, SW, and NW radii. Use nautical miles, or choose km. If you have the radius of the outermost closed isobar (ROCI) instead, select ROCI; this calculator estimates R34 from it.

  4. Add forward motion and read the result

    Forward speed is how fast the storm’s center moves, in knots. Results update immediately. The pressure deficit is environmental pressure minus central pressure. Open Calculation details to follow the numbers.

How do I average the TS radius?

For radii of 120, 100, 80, and 100 nmi, enter (120 + 100 + 80 + 100) ÷ 4 = 100 nmi. A reported zero is left out of the average; a missing quadrant is unknown. A single “winds extend up to” distance is a maximum extent, rather than this quadrant average. Enter 0 only when there are no TS-force winds.

Before using a result

Use an analyzed environmental pressure and radius when available; the starting values are examples. R34 is a radius, not the eye diameter or radius of maximum wind. Pressure → wind can show several solutions. An estimate from the relationship can differ from an observed storm.

About the methods

CKZ uses the ROCI size rule inferred from the supplied KnackBot REVCKZ results. It uses S = ROCI / 480 + 0.1, with ROCI in nmi, then solves CKZ equation 7a at 18° absolute latitude and under, or 7b above 18°. Motion is corrected by 1.5 × C0.63, with C in knots. This reproduces the supplied REVCKZ results to four decimals, including both hemispheres and ROCI values of 330, 440 and 600 nmi. KnackBot’s source has not been verified; behavior at other sizes and a possible size floor remain unconfirmed. CKZ supports Wind → pressure, Pressure → wind, and CI → pressure. Both wind–pressure directions use the same equation; CI is first converted to wind. The published R34 CKZ and CKK options use their own size calculations.

CKK · Chavas, Knaff & Klotzbach (2025) predicts central pressure from 1-minute maximum wind, azimuthal-mean R34, latitude, forward speed, and environmental pressure. It uses the final published equation 5 and the author’s motion correction, Vmean = Vmax − 0.55 × forward speed. Winds are converted to m/s and radius to meters internally. For reported quadrant radii, average the nonzero values and choose the × 0.85 convention; if your radius is already an azimuthal mean, choose use directly. ROCI is not an input to this model. Environmental pressure is used as entered; POCI + 2 mb is a proxy when an analyzed environment is unavailable. The model was fitted to aircraft pressure observations in the southwestern North Atlantic and eastern North Pacific during 2004–2022. Pressure → wind numerically inverts the same equation; CI → pressure first converts an existing CI number to wind. The calculator covers 25–250 kt, or at least 34 kt with nonzero R34; this extends beyond the fitting sample.

Dvorak (1984) uses the traditional CI-to-wind and basin pressure tables published by NHC. Enter an existing current intensity (CI) number from 1.0 to 8.0, a 1-minute wind from 25 to 170 kt, or a pressure within the selected table. Intermediate values are linearly interpolated; pressure is unavailable below CI 2.0, and 25 kt corresponds to CI 1.0–1.5. The complete reference table appears in Calculation details. Deriving CI from satellite imagery requires a Dvorak analysis; during weakening, CI can differ from the cloud-pattern T-number.

SCH · Schloemer / Holland pressure profile estimates central pressure from an observed station sea-level pressure, its distance from the center, environmental pressure, and RMW. It solves P(r) = Pc + (Pe − Pc) exp[−(RMW / r)B] for Pc. B = 1 gives the original Schloemer (1954) profile; other B values use Holland’s generalization. The assumed profile is circularly symmetric. Uncertainty in distance, RMW, B, and the station reading affects the estimate, especially far from the center.

Brown, Franklin & Landsea (2006) fitted Atlantic reconnaissance-based best-track observations from 1998–2005. Choose the overall fit or a separate latitude, 12-hour wind-trend, or regional fit. Each uses V = a(b − Pc)c, with V in knots and Pc in mb. The fitted constant b is not an environmental-pressure measurement. The latitude subsets describe north and south of 25° N within the Atlantic sample.

CKZ · Published R34 equations uses Courtney & Knaff’s (2009) equation 7a below 18° and equation 7b at 18° and above. CKZ · Original equation only uses the original pressure equation (equation 1 / 7b) at every latitude, with the same R34 size estimate and editable storm motion. Both accept 1-minute winds in knots, mph, or km/h; inputs are converted to knots before calculation. Radius inputs are converted to nautical miles before estimating R34 from ROCI. The environmental pressure entered is used directly; add 2 mb yourself when starting from POCI. ADT / McIDAS-V reproduces SSEC’s documented CKZ conversion, including its 11-knot motion default and weak-system pressure adjustment.

All wind results are 1-minute sustained estimates. The CI option for published R34 CKZ and ADT starts from an existing intensity number; satellite imagery is needed to derive CI itself. Brown’s equation is inverted algebraically for Wind → pressure; published R34 CKZ and ADT use a numerical solver for Pressure → wind. More than one CKZ/ADT solution can exist. Brown, published R34 CKZ, and ADT cover 25–250 kt, with a minimum of 34 kt when a TS-force radius is present. This calculation range extends beyond the Brown study’s observations; values below 892 mb, or below 920 mb for its Atlantic sub-basin fit, are extrapolations. These are empirical estimates; real storms can depart from the relationship.
