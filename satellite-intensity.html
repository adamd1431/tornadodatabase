Methods, training data & limits

The boosted regression trees use measured temperatures, the image-derived Raw T diagnostic, infrared eye structure and strictly earlier six-hour thermal history. They learn residual corrections to empirical temperature/wind–pressure priors. Final T, CI, official winds and recon winds are never inference features. Labels are NOAA IBTrACS USA 1-minute best-track winds and central pressures, interpolated only across gaps of six hours or less. Official intensity is never an input feature.

SI-6 measures the calibrated image itself: warm-eye area, radial cloud temperatures, cold-cloud coverage, ring completeness and asymmetry. A storm-balanced ridge model learns residual corrections to the Western Pacific trees, with each wind/pressure head independently gated on validation and a previously unused year. Out-of-range or incomplete images retain SI-5. The first candidate did not pass the release checks; both corrections are disabled and SI-5 predictions remain active. No storm name, identity, MOTIS pressure or personal estimate enters this correction. The SI-5 archive benchmark above describes its original model; the native-image evaluation is reported separately.

SI-5 removes the SI-4 MODIS replacement path. The model now reads infrared measurements, image-derived structure and strictly earlier thermal context. MOTIS, official records and your saved estimates appear solely as comparison evidence; none can change a prediction or select its peak. A separate Western Pacific boosted-tree component is fitted on 2013–2015 and 2017–2021, calibrated on 2022, selected on 2023 and evaluated on 2024. The entire 2016 season is a separate diagnostic holdout, including Meranti and Nepartak. These labels are best track, not independent reconnaissance truth.

SI-3 adds 64 measured native-image training examples from the hourly development checks. Labels remain time-matched best track; your lifetime peak values are comparison targets and never become per-hour labels. Entire seasons are separated for training, calibration, model selection and final retrospective evaluation. Training includes completed 2025 data; the 2024 test is a withheld-season retrospective comparison, not a chronological operational hindcast. Model settings are chosen on the validation season. Reported errors describe the held-out archive measurement benchmark; local pixel sampling, center choice and satellite resolution introduce additional differences. Best track can itself incorporate satellite estimates, so this benchmark is not independent aircraft validation.

The displayed baseline uses the SSEC ADT 9.1 guide’s eye-scene coefficients and annulus sampling, not the complete operational ADT history/scene algorithm. Released trees retain their frozen 8.2.1 sampler and previously fitted weights. The two Raw T diagnostics are labeled separately; changing a formula does not refit a trained model. CKZ pressure depends on the estimated wind and measured/reported outer structure. Its value is shown separately rather than treated as an independent machine-learning prediction. These are empirical regressions, not a first-principles atmosphere model. SST and shear are displayed as environmental context when available; the tree model was not trained on them.

Non-eye, land-contaminated, incomplete or unclear scenes may produce no estimate. No storm-name corrections, forced Category 5 upgrades or extrapolated table pressures are applied. RMW requires separate observations or a separately validated retrieval; the cold ring is never relabeled as RMW.
